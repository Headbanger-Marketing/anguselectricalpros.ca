Before work begins

Work out where a problem starts before you call anyone.

A circuit can reach beyond the room it serves. In a detached house with an attached garage, the panel-to-garage route can carry lights and receptacles that share circuits with indoor rooms, so a dead garage outlet may point to something wider.

Write down what stopped, what stayed on and whether neighbours or street lights were affected. That record helps the contractor and the utility ask the right questions, and it avoids guesses about where the cause sits.

How can I tell if the utility or my own wiring caused a power loss?

A homeowner cannot always tell, and nothing should be opened to find out. Note which rooms and devices lost power, whether neighbours did too, and share that with the utility and a Licensed Electrical Contractor.

What should I photograph before calling a contractor about flickering lights?

Photograph the affected fixtures and the closed panel door with its labels, from the outside only. Add a written note of the time, the rooms involved and which appliances were running, without touching any equipment.

Can a garage outlet that stops working affect rooms in the house?

It can, where garage receptacles or lights share a circuit with indoor rooms. Do not trace circuits yourself. Tell the contractor which spaces lost power together so the assessment starts from that pattern.

Who should I call first when only part of the house lost power?

If neighbours are also affected, contact the utility first. If only part of your home lost power, a Licensed Electrical Contractor can assess it, but lights that dim or glow brighter than usual can point to the supply, so report that to the utility as well.
